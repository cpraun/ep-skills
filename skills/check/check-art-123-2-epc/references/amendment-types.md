# Taxonomy of amendment types and their specific tests

Part of the `check-art-123-2-epc` skill. Read the section for the amendment type(s) identified in Step 1.

## A. Deletion of features (Streichung von Merkmalen)

- **Three criteria (Guidelines H-V, 3.1; originating in T 331/87).** The removed or replaced feature (1) was not explained as essential in the application as filed, (2) is not, as such, indispensable for the function of the invention, and (3) its removal requires no modification of other features to compensate. Failing one criterion infringes Art. 123(2); meeting all three is not sufficient — the Gold Standard still applies.
- Deleting a feature that is structurally or functionally connected to retained features raises a strong presumption of added matter.
- Deletion may also raise Art. 123(3) EPC; flag it if relevant.

## B. Generalisation of a feature (Verallgemeinerung)

- The generalised wording must itself be directly and unambiguously derivable; obviousness or technical equivalence is not enough.
- Admissible if the application describes the general concept and the specific example is merely illustrative; inadmissible if only the specific instance is disclosed, or if the generalisation introduces a new technical concept.

## C. Intermediate generalisation (Zwischenverallgemeinerung)

- A feature is taken from a specific embodiment into a broader claim, while other features presented together with it as an integrated unit are omitted.
- **Functional-inseparability test** (T 962/98; Guidelines H-V, 3.2.1): were the retained and omitted features disclosed as an inseparable combination (funktional zusammengehörige Merkmalskombination), or was the retained feature independently disclosed at the claimed level of generality?
- Red flags: the feature appears in only one example; the description ties it structurally or functionally to other features of that embodiment; the claim omits surrounding features the skilled person would regard as part of the same inventive concept.
- A general passage (e.g., preceding the embodiments) describing the feature at the claimed level can provide basis.

## D. Selection from lists (Auswahl aus Listen)

- **Single list:** generally admissible if the list is finite, the selection is complete (not a sub-list), and the original disclosure does not single out the item in a way inconsistent with the claim.
- **Multiple independent selections (Mehrfachauswahl):** a combination of selections from two or more lists is admissible only if the original disclosure presents that specific combination, explicitly or as a coherent unit (T 727/00, T 686/99).
- **Pointer:** a specific pointer in the original disclosure (e.g., "particularly preferred is X combined with Y") may support admissibility; a post-hoc explanation by the applicant does not.

## E. Numerical range amendments (Zahlenwertbereiche)

- A narrowed range is admissible if disclosed as such (e.g., a preferred sub-range) or directly and unambiguously derivable from examples plus general description (T 2/81, T 201/83).
- Combined end-points: a preferred range plus an adjoining part-range of the general range is disclosed (T 2/81); a range from the lower limit of the general range and the lower limit of the preferred range, which excludes the preferred range, is not (T 1170/02).
- A new end-point must be derivable; an arbitrary value between disclosed values is not, unless implicitly disclosed.
- A range taken from example values may be derivable if the description characterises the examples at that level of generality.

## F. Added features (Hinzufügen von Merkmalen)

- Check both that the feature is disclosed and that it is disclosed **in combination** with the remaining claim features. A feature disclosed only in isolation may not support the claimed combination.
- Basis may come from the description or drawings, not only from the original claims.

## G. Disclaimers (Disclaimer)

1. **Disclosed disclaimer (G 2/10):** assessed by the Gold Standard. Admissible if the subject-matter remaining after the disclaimer is directly and unambiguously disclosed in the application as filed.
2. **Undisclosed disclaimer (G 1/03, G 2/03, G 1/16):** the Gold Standard does not apply (G 1/16); it is admissible only
   - to restore novelty over an accidental anticipation (Art. 54(2)), over a conflicting application (Art. 54(3)), or to exclude subject-matter excluded under Art. 53 EPC;
   - if it removes no more than necessary to restore novelty; and
   - if it does not become relevant for inventive step and provides no technical contribution (G 1/16).

   Outside these situations, an undisclosed disclaimer violates Art. 123(2) EPC.

## H. Change of claim category (Kategorienänderung)

- The reformulated claim must be directly and unambiguously derivable from the original disclosure.
- Scrutinise the scope: a product-by-process or use claim may implicitly define structural or functional features not disclosed for the original product or process.
