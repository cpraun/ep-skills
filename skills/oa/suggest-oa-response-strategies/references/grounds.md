# Ground-specific standards

Part of the `suggest-oa-response-strategies` skill. Read the section for every ground the Examiner raised, before Phase 1. For each ground, read the named skill's `SKILL.md` (and its bundled references) and apply its standard; do not paraphrase the law from memory. Use the skills internally — do not produce a separate deliverable per ground.

## Contents
- Art. 54 — novelty
- Art. 56 — inventive step
- Art. 83 — sufficiency
- Art. 84 — clarity
- Art. 123(2) — added matter
- Art. 76(1) — divisional basis

## Art. 54 — novelty (`check-art-54-epc`)

- **Merit:** Strong if every feature is directly and unambiguously disclosed in the cited passages; Weak if at least one feature is missing or the Examiner mosaicked embodiments of D1; Subjective only where claim interpretation (G 1/24) is genuinely contested.
- **Argument lines:** feature not disclosed in the cited passage; implicit disclosure not directly and unambiguously derivable; mosaicking; sub-range sufficiently far removed (T 261/15).

## Art. 56 — inventive step (no dedicated skill)

- Apply the problem–solution approach (Guidelines G-VII, 5): closest prior art, distinguishing feature, objective technical problem, could-would.
- **Merit:** Strong if all four steps hold against the application; Weak if the closest prior art is wrong, the distinguishing feature mischaracterised, the problem formulated with hindsight, or no pointer motivates the combination.
- **Argument lines:** the same defects, plus a missing technical effect or hindsight reconstruction.

## Art. 83 — sufficiency (`check-art-83-epc`)

- **Merit:** Strong if the disclosure plainly does not enable the claim across its whole scope (T 409/91; reach-through claims T 1063/06; undue burden T 435/91); Subjective if it turns on G 2/21 plausibility.
- **Argument lines:** the gap is filled by common general knowledge or the worked examples; plausibility under G 2/21 is satisfied by the application as filed.
- **Amendment check:** a newly narrowed range or an imported functional feature must not break whole-scope enablement.

## Art. 84 — clarity (`check-art-84-epc`)

- **Merit:** Strong for a textbook defect (relative term without reference point, parameter without measurement method, mixed category, missing essential feature; Guidelines F-IV); Weak if the term is well recognised in the art or defined in the claim.
- **Phase 2 amendments:** import explicit clarifying statements from the description, quoted literally with citation. If the claim is clear to a mind willing to understand, prefer argument only and quote the supporting passage.
- **Amendment check:** re-run the clarity check on the amended wording — an imported parameter without measurement method or a "preferably" carried over creates a new objection.

## Art. 123(2) — added matter (`check-art-123-2-epc`)

- **Merit:** Strong if no direct and unambiguous basis exists (G 2/10); Weak if the basis can be cited verbatim and the combination is disclosed as a coherent unit; Subjective where the functional-inseparability test for an intermediate generalisation decides.
- **Amendment check (every route):** cite the literal basis; for description-sourced features, run the intermediate-generalisation test (T 962/98; Guidelines H-V, 3.2.1); for picks from several lists, the multiple-selection test (T 727/00, T 686/99); for ranges, T 2/81 / T 1170/02.

## Art. 76(1) — divisional basis (`check-art-76-1-epc`)

- Apply the Gold Standard against the parent **as filed** (G 1/05, G 1/06); for a divisional, every amendment also needs basis in each earlier application.
